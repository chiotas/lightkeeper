Lightkeeper

A calm Home screen for LG TVs on older webOS, to use with GlassHouse. It shows the time, the weather for one city and a short row of apps and HDMI inputs over a photograph or a painting, and keeps out LG's ads and suggestions.

  • It opens with the Home key, when the TV comes on, and when Back leaves the last app. Down opens LG's own Home.
  • You set it up from your phone at http://<your TV's address>:8090, with a live preview: up to fifty tiles, names, white logos or app icons, the device or a plain TV icon on each input, nineteen backgrounds, ten of them seascapes by Ivan Aivazovsky, or your own pictures, one at random each time the TV turns on if you like, the clock, the weather and an optional PIN.
  • After installing it, open Lightkeeper once. It asks the Homebrew Channel to set up its settings server and start-up hook, which takes about ten seconds.
  • New versions show up here: open Lightkeeper in this list and choose Update. Your settings stay.

You need root, the Homebrew Channel and GlassHouse, which hands the Home key to Lightkeeper. Tested on an LG C1 with webOS 6.5.3 and GlassHouse 0.118.0, and reported working on a C8 with webOS 4.4.3. It should work on webOS 4 to 8. On webOS 2 and 3 it works without the Home key, which those TVs cannot hand over; the project page has another way to map a button. Tried it on another TV? The project page has a short form for reports.

To remove it, choose Remove Lightkeeper in the settings page, or uninstall it here: the Home key goes back to LG's Home.
